Circuit identification and component values

02DC operating point

The Q-point

At DC every capacitor is an open circuit. The bias network then fixes $I_C$, $V_{CE}$ and the small-signal transconductance $g_m$.

The divider $R_{B1}$, $R_{B2}$ is replaced by its Thevenin equivalent:

$$\begin{aligned} V_{BB} &= V_{CC}\,\frac{R_{B2}}{R_{B1} + R_{B2}} = 5 \cdot \frac{100\,\text{k}}{300\,\text{k}} = 1.667\,\text{V} \\[4pt] R_{BB} &= R_{B1} \parallel R_{B2} = \frac{200\,\text{k} \cdot 100\,\text{k}}{300\,\text{k}} = 66.67\,\text{k}\Omega \end{aligned}$$

With $V_{BE} \approx 0.7\,\text{V}$ and $\beta = 200$, the base loop gives the collector current and the collector loop gives $V_{CE}$:

$$\begin{aligned} I_C &\approx \frac{V_{BB} - V_{BE}}{R_E + R_{BB}/\beta} = \frac{0.967}{500 + 333.5} = 1.160\,\text{mA} \\[4pt] V_{CE} &= V_{CC} - I_C\,(R_C + R_E) = 5 - 2.90 = 2.10\,\text{V} \end{aligned}$$

$V_{CE} = 2.10\,\text{V}$ exceeds $V_{CE(\text{sat})} = 0.2\,\text{V}$, so the transistor is in the forward-active region and the Q-point is valid. The small-signal parameters follow from $I_C$ with $V_T = 26\,\text{mV}$:

$$g_m = \frac{I_C}{V_T} = \frac{1.160\,\text{mA}}{26\,\text{mV}} = 44.62\,\text{mA/V}, \qquad r_\pi = \frac{\beta}{g_m} = 4.48\,\text{k}\Omega$$
$I_C$ is set by the base divider and $R_E$, not by $R_C$. Raising $R_C$ steepens the load line until the operating point reaches $V_{CE(\text{sat})}$; beyond that the model holds the gain at its edge-of-saturation value and reports the stage as saturated.

03small-signal model

AC equivalent circuit and midband gain

The AC model replaces the BJT with its hybrid-π model, connects $V_{CC}$ to ground, and keeps each capacitor as the impedance $Z = 1/(sC)$.

  • $V_{CC}$ to ground: the supply is an AC ground, so $R_{B1}$ and $R_{B2}$ appear in parallel as $R_{BB}$ and $R_C$ returns to ground.
  • BJT to hybrid-π: $r_\pi$ between base and emitter, and a current source $g_m v_\pi$ from collector to emitter.
  • capacitors retained as $Z_C = 1/(sC)$.

The equivalent circuit has three capacitor-dependent paths. At the input, $v_s$ drives $R_S$, $C_{C1}$ and $R_{BB} \parallel r_\pi \approx 4.20\,\text{k}\Omega$, so $C_{C1}$ forms a high-pass filter with the total resistance it sees. At the emitter, $R_E$ is in parallel with $C_E$: at low frequency $R_E$ provides negative feedback and reduces the gain, and as frequency rises $C_E$ bypasses $R_E$ and the gain increases. At the output, the collector node drives $R_L$ through $C_{C2}$, a second high-pass filter.

At midband the coupling and bypass capacitors are short circuits and the transistor capacitances are open. With $R_C' = R_C \parallel R_L = 1\,\text{k}\Omega$:

$$\begin{aligned} R_\text{in} &= R_{BB} \parallel r_\pi = \frac{66.67\,\text{k} \cdot 4.48\,\text{k}}{66.67\,\text{k} + 4.48\,\text{k}} \approx 4.20\,\text{k}\Omega \\[4pt] A_\text{mid} &= -g_m R_C' \,\frac{R_\text{in}}{R_S + R_\text{in}} = -44.62\,\text{mA/V} \cdot 1\,\text{k}\Omega \cdot \frac{4200}{4300} = -43.6\,\text{V/V} \end{aligned}$$

This is $32.8\,\text{dB}$; the minus sign is the $180^\circ$ inversion of the common-emitter stage.

04transfer function

Poles, zero and the Bode plot

$A_V(s) = V_o(s)/V_s(s)$ is the midband gain multiplied by one frequency-dependent term for each capacitor.

Low-frequency poles

Each coupling capacitor sees the resistance on both of its sides. $C_E$ sees $R_E$ in parallel with the resistance looking into the emitter, with $R_{th} = R_S \parallel R_{BB} \approx 100\,\Omega$:

$$\begin{aligned} \omega_{L1} &= \frac{1}{C_{C1}(R_S + R_{BB} \parallel r_\pi)} = \frac{1}{0.1\,\mu \cdot 4300} = 2326\,\text{rad/s} &&(f_{L1} \approx 370\,\text{Hz}) \\[4pt] \omega_{L2} &= \frac{1}{C_{C2}(R_C + R_L)} = \frac{1}{0.1\,\mu \cdot 4\,\text{k}} = 2500\,\text{rad/s} &&(f_{L2} \approx 398\,\text{Hz}) \\[4pt] \omega_{L3} &= \frac{1}{C_E\left(R_E \parallel \dfrac{r_\pi + R_{th}}{\beta + 1}\right)} = \frac{1}{220\,\mu \cdot 21.8} = 208.5\,\text{rad/s} &&(f_{L3} \approx 33\,\text{Hz}) \\[4pt] \omega_{Z} &= \frac{1}{C_E R_E} = \frac{1}{220\,\mu \cdot 500} = 9.09\,\text{rad/s} &&(f_{Z} \approx 1.4\,\text{Hz}) \end{aligned}$$

High-frequency poles and the Miller effect

The transistor capacitances follow from $f_T = 300\,\text{MHz}$. $C_\mu$ bridges the inverting gain from base to collector, so it appears at the input multiplied by $1 + g_m R_C'$:

$$\begin{aligned} C_\pi &= \frac{g_m}{2\pi f_T} - C_\mu = 23.7 - 4 = 19.7\,\text{pF} \\[4pt] C_\text{in,Miller} &= C_\pi + C_\mu(1 + g_m R_C') = 19.7 + 4 \times 45.6 = 202.1\,\text{pF} \end{aligned}$$

With $R_{th,b} = R_S \parallel R_{BB} \parallel r_\pi \approx 97.7\,\Omega$ at the base and $R_C'$ at the collector:

$$\omega_{H1} = \frac{1}{R_{th,b}\,C_\text{in,Miller}} \;(f_{H1} \approx 8.06\,\text{MHz}), \qquad \omega_{H2} = \frac{1}{R_C' C_\mu} \;(f_{H2} \approx 39.8\,\text{MHz})$$

Complete transfer function

$$A_V(s) = A_\text{mid}\cdot\frac{s}{s + \omega_{L1}}\cdot\frac{s}{s + \omega_{L2}}\cdot \frac{s + \omega_Z}{s + \omega_{L3}}\cdot\frac{\omega_{H1}}{s + \omega_{H1}}\cdot\frac{\omega_{H2}}{s + \omega_{H2}}$$
Below $\omega_Z$ the bypass capacitor is ineffective, and the emitter term alone limits the gain to $g_m R_C'/(1 + g_m R_E) \approx 1.91$, or $5.6\,\text{dB}$; the bypass capacitor therefore provides about $27\,\text{dB}$ of midband gain. The high-frequency response is limited by the Miller effect: $C_\text{in,Miller} \approx 202\,\text{pF}$ is about ten times the intrinsic $C_\pi$.

The low-frequency $-3\,\text{dB}$ point is set mainly by the coupling capacitor poles $f_{L1} \approx 370\,\text{Hz}$ and $f_{L2} \approx 398\,\text{Hz}$. The high-frequency response is limited by the Miller pole at $f_{H1} \approx 8.06\,\text{MHz}$. In the midband the phase is flat at $-180^\circ$.

The Nyquist plot traces $A_V(j\omega)$ in the complex plane as $\omega$ increases. The locus starts at the origin at $\omega = 0$, reaches the negative real axis at midband, where the phase is close to $-180^\circ$ and the magnitude is $|A_\text{mid}|$, and returns towards the origin at high frequency. The faint curve is its mirror image for negative frequencies.

The cursor frequency marks the same point on the Bode and Nyquist plots. Its vector has length $|A_V|$ and makes the angle $\angle A_V$, measured from $0^\circ$ to $360^\circ$ counter-clockwise from the positive real axis.

The midband gain is about $32.8\,\text{dB}$. The coupling poles cause the low-frequency roll-off and the Miller pole dominates the high-frequency roll-off; on the Nyquist plot these appear as the two halves of the loop on either side of the midband point.
