Doping the silicon lattice

Pure silicon at room temperature has too few free carriers to be useful in a device. Replacing a small fraction of its atoms with group V or group III elements adds electrons or holes in controlled numbers, sets which carrier dominates conduction, and moves the Fermi level towards the conduction or valence band.

01the doped lattice

Substitutional doping of the silicon crystal

Silicon crystallises in the diamond cubic lattice. Each atom forms four tetrahedral covalent bonds with its nearest neighbours, and each bond holds one shared pair of electrons. At low temperature every valence electron is held in a bond, and the crystal does not conduct.

A dopant atom takes the place of a silicon atom in the lattice:

  • phosphorus (group V) has five valence electrons. Four complete the bonds to its neighbours; the fifth is bound only weakly and is released at room temperature, leaving a fixed $\text{P}^+$ ion. Phosphorus is a donor and the material is n-type;
  • boron (group III) has three valence electrons, so one of its four bonds lacks an electron. A neighbouring bond electron can move into the vacancy, which moves the hole and leaves a fixed $\text{B}^-$ ion. Boron is an acceptor and the material is p-type;
  • pure silicon gains carriers only by thermal generation: a bond breaks, and an electron and a hole appear together, later to recombine.

A voltage across the crystal sets up a field $\vec{E}$ directed from the positive to the negative plate. Electrons drift against the field towards the positive plate and holes drift with it. The external circuit supplies electrons at the negative plate and removes them at the positive plate, so the current is sustained. The conductivity,

$$\sigma = q\,(n\,\mu_n + p\,\mu_p),$$

is set by the carrier densities $n$ and $p$ and the mobilities $\mu_n$ and $\mu_p$; it does not depend on the applied voltage.

The dopant fraction is exaggerated for visibility. Practical doping is $10^{14}$ to $10^{19}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$, about $2\times10^{-9}$ to $2\times10^{-4}$ of the $5\times10^{22}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$ silicon atoms. The animation shows holes as vacancies passed between bonds near the boron sites; physically the hole is a mobile carrier that drifts towards the negative plate while the $\text{B}^-$ ion stays fixed.

02dopant energy levels

Donor and acceptor levels in the band gap

A dopant adds a localised state inside the band gap. The fifth electron of phosphorus occupies a donor level $E_D$ about $45\,\text{meV}$ below the conduction band edge. The empty bond of boron forms an acceptor level $E_A$ about $45\,\text{meV}$ above the valence band edge.

Both energies are comparable to $kT \approx 25.9\,\text{meV}$ at $300\,\text{K}$, so almost every donor gives its electron to the conduction band and almost every acceptor takes an electron from the valence band. This is the assumption of complete ionisation.

The Fermi level $E_F$, the energy at which a state has an occupation probability of one half, moves with the doping. In pure silicon it lies near the middle of the gap. In doped material,

$$E_C - E_F = kT \ln\frac{N_C}{N_D}\quad(\text{n-type}),\qquad E_F - E_V = kT \ln\frac{N_V}{N_A}\quad(\text{p-type}),$$

so $E_F$ approaches the conduction band edge as the donor concentration $N_D$ rises and the valence band edge as the acceptor concentration $N_A$ rises.

The diagram places $E_D$ and $E_A$ at 15 % of the gap from the band edges so that they can be seen. At $45\,\text{meV}$ they would lie at about 4 % of the $1.12\,\text{eV}$ gap.

03carrier concentrations

Majority and minority carriers

In thermal equilibrium the product of the electron and hole concentrations is fixed by the material and the temperature, independent of the doping. This is the law of mass action:

$$n\,p = n_i^2$$

The crystal is electrically neutral, so with all dopants ionised

$$n + N_A = p + N_D.$$

Solving the two together for donor doping gives

$$n = \frac{N_D}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{N_D^2}{4} + n_i^2},\qquad p = \frac{n_i^2}{n},$$

and the corresponding result with $n$ and $p$ exchanged for acceptor doping. Once $N_D \gg n_i$, $n \approx N_D$ and $p \approx n_i^2 / N_D$. With $n_i = 10^{10}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$ at $300\,\text{K}$, a donor concentration of $10^{16}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$ gives $10^{16}$ electrons and only $10^{4}$ holes per cubic centimetre. The Fermi level sits above the intrinsic level $E_i$ by

$$E_F - E_i = kT \ln\frac{n}{n_i}.$$
Electrons are the majority carriers in n-type material and holes in p-type material. The minority carriers are scarce, but they control the diffusion currents of the pn junction.
