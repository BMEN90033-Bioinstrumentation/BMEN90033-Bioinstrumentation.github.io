BMEN90033 · WEEK 9 · SAMPLE-AND-HOLD

The sample-and-hold circuit

A successive-approximation ADC resolves one bit per clock cycle. If the input changes during conversion, the comparator decisions made early in the sequence no longer correspond to the value at the comparator at the end. The sample-and-hold circuit prevents this by capturing the input on a small capacitor and presenting the captured value to the converter for the full duration of the conversion.

Motivation
01need for a held sample

A SAR ADC requires a stationary input

The successive-approximation algorithm refines a digital estimate of $V_\mathrm{in}$ one bit at a time. Each comparison sets one bit of the output, and that bit remains fixed in all subsequent cycles. The algorithm is therefore valid only if the same $V_\mathrm{in}$ is presented to the comparator on every cycle.

The adjacent panel runs an $N=8$ bit SAR conversion on a slowly drifting input. With the sample-and-hold disabled, each comparison is made against a different input value, and the final code differs from the value the input had at the start of the conversion. With the sample-and-hold enabled, the input is captured at the start of the conversion and the algorithm converges on the captured value.

The S/H control switches between the two cases. With the sample-and-hold enabled, the final code corresponds to the captured value; with it disabled, the code error grows with the input slew.

The maximum input slew that a SAR converter tolerates without a front-end S/H is approximately $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}t < \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{LSB} / T_\mathrm{conv}$. For a 12-bit converter running at $\,1\,\mathrm{MS\,s^{-1}}$ on a $1\,\mathrm{V}$ range, this is about $0.12\,\mathrm{V\,ms^{-1}}$. A QRS complex amplified to full scale reaches only about $0.05\,\mathrm{V\,ms^{-1}}$, but multiplexed channels, fast transients, and higher-bandwidth signals exceed this limit.

Track and hold
02two operating phases

The track phase and the hold phase

A sample-and-hold alternates between two phases under the control of a clock $\phi$. During the track phase, a switch closes and a small hold capacitor $C_H$ follows the input. During the hold phase, the switch opens and the capacitor retains the voltage it had at the moment of opening, because no path remains through which charge can flow.

The capacitor voltage is therefore a piecewise function: a continuous segment that follows $V_\mathrm{in}$ during track, and a flat segment at the captured value during hold. The downstream ADC performs its conversion entirely within the hold phase.

The sliders set the input frequency, the sample rate and the fraction of the period spent tracking. As the sample rate increases, the staircase of held values approaches the input more closely.

If the track phase is shorter than the time required for $C_H$ to settle, the captured value is biased towards the previous sample. The minimum track time is set by the acquisition time derived in the next section.

03circuit topology

Two buffers, a switch and a hold capacitor

The standard sample-and-hold places a series MOSFET switch between two unity-gain buffers. The input buffer drives the switch from a low source impedance so that the hold capacitor charges quickly. The output buffer presents a high impedance to the capacitor, so that during hold the only discharge paths are the input bias current of the buffer and the leakage of the switch.

The hold capacitor $C_H$ is connected between the switch output and ground. Its value is a compromise: a larger $C_H$ reduces droop and is less affected by charge injection, but takes longer to acquire the input.

Click the gate of the MOSFET to toggle the clock manually. Charging current flows into $C_H$ only while the switch is closed; in the hold phase the capacitor is isolated and $v_C$ remains constant.

In practice the switch is implemented as a transmission gate (an N-channel and a P-channel MOSFET in parallel) so that the on-resistance remains approximately constant across the full input range. The principle of operation is unchanged.

04acquisition time

Charging $C_H$ through the on-resistance

When the switch closes onto an input that has changed since the previous track phase, the hold capacitor charges through the on-resistance of the switch. The capacitor voltage follows $$ V_C(t) = V_\mathrm{in} \left( 1 - e^{-t/\tau} \right), \quad \tau = R_\mathrm{on}\,C_H. $$

The acquisition time $t_\mathrm{acq}$ is the time required for $V_C$ to settle within an acceptable fraction of the final value. For an $N$-bit ADC, settling to within $\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{LSB}$ requires $t_\mathrm{acq} \approx \tau (N + 1) \ln 2$. Track phases shorter than this leave the capacitor at a value biased towards the previous sample.

The time constant $\tau$ scales the charging curve in proportion to $R_\mathrm{on}$ and $C_H$. The readout reports the maximum sample rate consistent with full settling within a $30\%$ track duty.

A small $C_H$ acquires quickly but droops faster in hold; a large $C_H$ droops slowly but limits the sample rate. Typical hold capacitors in monolithic S/H amplifiers are $1$ to $10\,\mathrm{pF}$.

05non-idealities

Droop, aperture jitter and charge injection

A real sample-and-hold departs from the ideal in three ways. Each error is small in isolation; together they set the practical resolution and sample rate of the front end.

Droop

During hold, the capacitor discharges slowly through the leakage current of the switch and the input bias of the output buffer. The held voltage falls at a rate $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}t = I_\mathrm{leak} / C_H$. Across a conversion window of $T_\mathrm{conv}$, this produces a droop of $\Delta V = I_\mathrm{leak}\,T_\mathrm{conv} / C_H$.

Aperture jitter

The clock edge that opens the switch carries timing noise. If the input is changing at $\mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}t$ at the moment of opening, a jitter $\sigma_t$ on the clock translates to a voltage error $\sigma_V = \sigma_t \cdot \mathrm{d}V/\mathrm{d}t$. Aperture jitter limits the achievable signal-to-noise ratio of high-frequency converters.

Charge injection

When the MOSFET turns off, channel charge is injected onto $C_H$. This produces a small step $\Delta V_\mathrm{cj} \approx Q_\mathrm{ch} / (2 C_H)$ on every hold transition. A dummy switch of half the size, driven by the inverted clock, cancels most of the injected charge.

06feeding the converter

The held value is the converter input

The output of the sample-and-hold is the input to the converter. At the SAR input, $V_\mathrm{in}$ is constant for the entire conversion window, regardless of how the true input changes. The staircase shown in the panel is the sequence of held values, each delivered to the converter for one full sample period.

The next page makes this assumption implicitly. The sampling, quantisation and SAR algorithm scenes in Analogue-to-digital conversion all operate on the held output, and the raw input does not appear.

The combination of a sample-and-hold and a SAR ADC is common enough that monolithic SAR converters integrate the S/H on the same die, often as a charge-redistribution capacitor array that serves as both the sampling capacitor and the conversion DAC.
