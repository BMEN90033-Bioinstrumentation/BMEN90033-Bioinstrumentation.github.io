Myelination and saltatory conduction

In an unmyelinated axon the action potential is regenerated at every point of the membrane and the wavefront moves continuously. In a myelinated axon the membrane is covered by insulating myelin except at the nodes of Ranvier, so excitation is regenerated only at the nodes and the wavefront advances from node to node. This page compares the two modes of conduction, the extracellular dipole at each wavefront, and the potential recorded at a fixed point on each fibre.

Continuous and saltatory propagation

Both axons are stimulated at their left ends at the same instant. On the lower, unmyelinated axon a depolarised region follows a wavefront that moves at constant speed. On the upper, myelinated axon the wavefront dwells briefly at each node of Ranvier and then moves rapidly to the next.

At each wavefront, intracellular current flows forward from the depolarised membrane into the resting membrane ahead and returns through the extracellular fluid. Viewed from outside, the front is a current dipole pointing in the direction of propagation: the positive pole faces the resting membrane ahead and the negative pole faces the depolarised membrane behind. The field arrows show the superposed extracellular field of the two dipoles.

Why myelin increases conduction velocity

Myelin is a multilayered wrapping of glial membrane, formed by Schwann cells in the peripheral nervous system and by oligodendrocytes in the central nervous system. It raises the transverse resistance of the internodal membrane and lowers its capacitance, each by a large factor.

Little current leaks across an internode, and little charge is needed to change its voltage, so the axial current from an active node brings the next node to threshold quickly. Voltage-gated sodium channels are concentrated at the nodes, and the action potential is regenerated only there.

In the model the front dwells at each node for $t_d = 0.08\,\text{s}/k$ and then crosses an internode of length $L$ in $t_j = 0.12\,\text{s}/k$, where $k$ is the speed setting. The unmyelinated front moves at the constant speed $v_u = 4k$ units/s. The average speed of the myelinated front, and its ratio to $v_u$, are

$$\bar v_m = \frac{L}{t_d + t_j}, \qquad \frac{\bar v_m}{v_u} = \frac{L}{(t_d + t_j)\, v_u}$$

The speed setting cancels from the ratio, which is about 8.75 for $L = 7$. The readout in section 01 gives the ratio of the simulated times each front takes to cross the axon; it is slightly lower because the last internode is shorter than $L$. Longer internodes give faster conduction in the model. In real fibres the internode length and the myelin thickness both scale with axon diameter. Conduction velocity rises approximately in proportion to diameter in myelinated fibres and approximately with the square root of diameter in unmyelinated fibres.

The membrane potential at a fixed point

A recording point $45$ units from the stimulated end of each axon samples the membrane potential as the wavefront passes. The waveform is a prescribed action potential shape $a(d)$, where $d$ is the distance from the recording point to the front, positive while the front has not yet arrived:

$$a(d) = \begin{cases} (1 - d/2)^2, & 0 < d \le 2 \\[4pt] -a_h + (1 + a_h)\,\dfrac{e^{d/1.5} - e^{-2}}{1 - e^{-2}}, & -3 < d \le 0 \\[8pt] -a_h\,\dfrac{e^{(d + 3)/3} - e^{-5/3}}{1 - e^{-5/3}}, & -8 < d \le -3 \\[6pt] 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
$$V_m = -70 + 110\, a(d)\ \text{mV}, \qquad a_h = 0.15$$

The repolarisation and recovery pieces are scaled so that $a(d)$ is continuous: it reaches the after-hyperpolarisation minimum $-a_h$ at $d = -3$ and returns to zero at $d = -8$.

The same spatial profile passes the recording point on both fibres, so the duration of the recorded potential scales inversely with the speed of the front. The myelinated fibre gives an early, brief event; the unmyelinated fibre a late, broad one. The myelinated front passes the recording point during a single jump between nodes, so the upstroke and most of the repolarisation are compressed into that jump, and the after-hyperpolarisation holds while the front dwells at the next node.

The readout gives the time at which each front reaches the recording point, and their ratio.

The waveform shape is prescribed, not computed from membrane dynamics. The membrane equations that produce the action potential are developed on the membrane patch page.
