The action potential in a membrane patch

The resting potential of an excitable cell arises from ion concentration gradients across the membrane and from the selective permeability of the membrane to each ion. A brief depolarising stimulus opens voltage-gated sodium channels; the regenerative sodium influx that follows, ended by sodium inactivation and a delayed potassium efflux, produces the action potential. This page runs the Hodgkin-Huxley model of a patch of squid giant axon membrane at $6.3\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, with an electrogenic sodium-potassium pump, and shows the channel gating and ion flux that underlie the membrane potential.

01the membrane patch

Ion gradients, channels and the pump

The scene shows a cross-section of a lipid bilayer separating the extracellular fluid (above) from the cytoplasm (below). The page models a squid giant axon, the preparation on which Hodgkin and Huxley based their model. Sodium is concentrated outside the axon and potassium inside. The concentrations are representative squid-axon values, with the external $\text{K}^+$ concentration chosen so that the Nernst potentials equal the reversal potentials of the model (section 02):

  • $\text{Na}^+$: $440\,\text{mM}$ outside, $55\,\text{mM}$ inside;
  • $\text{K}^+$: $16.3\,\text{mM}$ outside, $400\,\text{mM}$ inside.

Three membrane proteins span the bilayer. The voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ channel has four subunits that open with the activation variable $m$ and a cytoplasmic inactivation gate that moves into the pore as $h$ falls. The voltage-gated $\text{K}^+$ channel has four subunits that open with $n$. The $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase exports three $\text{Na}^+$ and imports two $\text{K}^+$ per ATP hydrolysed.

Ions cross at the rates set by the model (section 04). Passive flux, through the channel and the leak pathway of each ion, is drawn at that ion's channel; pump flux is drawn through the pump. At rest the pump exactly balances the passive leak of both ions, so the concentrations are stationary.

Each rendered particle represents 1000 ions crossing a $1\,\mu\text{m}^2$ patch, and the concentration readouts follow the amount of each ion in each compartment. The compartments in the scene are small, so one action potential visibly raises $[\text{Na}^+]_\text{in}$ and $[\text{K}^+]_\text{out}$; the pump returns them to their resting values over tens of seconds of animation. In a real axon the change per impulse is far smaller, and $E_\text{Na}$ and $E_\text{K}$ are held at their resting values.

02the membrane equation

The membrane as a capacitor with parallel conductances

The Hodgkin-Huxley model represents a patch of membrane as a capacitance $C_m$ in parallel with three ionic branches, each a conductance in series with the reversal potential of its ion. Conservation of charge gives

$$C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = I_\text{stim} - I_\text{Na} - I_\text{K} - I_\text{L}$$
$$\begin{aligned} I_\text{Na} &= \bar g_\text{Na}\, m^3 h\,(V - E_\text{Na}) \\ I_\text{K} &= \bar g_\text{K}\, n^4\,(V - E_\text{K}) \\ I_\text{L} &= g_\text{L}\,(V - E_\text{L}) \end{aligned}$$

The sodium and potassium reversal potentials are computed from the concentrations in section 01 with the Nernst equation, at the temperature of the Hodgkin-Huxley experiments, $T = 279.45\,\text{K}$ ($6.3\,{}^\circ\text{C}$):

$$E_X = \frac{RT}{zF}\ln\frac{[X]_o}{[X]_i}, \qquad \frac{RT}{F} = 24.08\,\text{mV}$$

with $z = +1$ for both ions. The remaining parameters are the standard squid-axon values, with the resting potential near $-65\,\text{mV}$:

  • $E_\text{Na} = 24.08\ln(440/55) = +50.1\,\text{mV}$, $E_\text{K} = 24.08\ln(16.3/400) = -77.1\,\text{mV}$;
  • $C_m = 1\,\mu\text{F/cm}^2$;
  • $\bar g_\text{Na} = 120$, $\bar g_\text{K} = 36$, $g_\text{L} = 0.3\,\text{mS/cm}^2$;
  • $E_\text{L} = -54.387\,\text{mV}$, the reversal potential of the lumped leak.

Section 04 shows how the leak is divided between $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{K}^+$ and combined with the pump current without changing this equation at rest.

The equations are integrated by the forward Euler method with $\Delta t = 0.05\,\text{ms}$, four steps per animation frame. The stimulus is a rectangular current pulse of $1\,\text{ms}$. With these settings a pulse of about $7\,\mu\text{A/cm}^2$ or more produces a full action potential that peaks near $+40\,\text{mV}$ and undershoots to about $-76\,\text{mV}$. A weaker pulse produces a small depolarisation that decays passively.

Above threshold the peak voltage changes by only a few millivolts with stimulus strength: the response is all-or-none. During the upstroke $V_m$ approaches $E_\text{Na}$, and during the after-hyperpolarisation it approaches $E_\text{K}$.

03channel gating

Voltage-dependent gating variables

Each gating variable $x \in \{m, h, n\}$ is the fraction of its gates in the permissive state. It relaxes towards a voltage-dependent steady state at voltage-dependent rates $\alpha_x$ (opening) and $\beta_x$ (closing), in $\text{ms}^{-1}$ with $V$ in mV:

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = \alpha_x(V)\,(1 - x) - \beta_x(V)\,x$$
$$\begin{aligned} \alpha_m &= \frac{0.1\,(V + 40)}{1 - e^{-(V + 40)/10}}, & \beta_m &= 4\,e^{-(V + 65)/18} \\[4pt] \alpha_h &= 0.07\,e^{-(V + 65)/20}, & \beta_h &= \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(V + 35)/10}} \\[4pt] \alpha_n &= \frac{0.01\,(V + 55)}{1 - e^{-(V + 55)/10}}, & \beta_n &= 0.125\,e^{-(V + 65)/80} \end{aligned}$$

The three variables act on different time scales, which sets the sequence of the action potential:

  • $m$ is fast. A depolarisation raises $m$, $g_\text{Na} = \bar g_\text{Na} m^3 h$ increases, and the inward sodium current depolarises the membrane further. This positive feedback is the upstroke.
  • $h$ is slower and falls with depolarisation, inactivating the sodium conductance.
  • $n$ is also slow and rises with depolarisation, so $g_\text{K} = \bar g_\text{K} n^4$ grows after the upstroke and the outward potassium current repolarises the membrane.
After an action potential $h$ is still low and $n$ still high. A second stimulus delivered during this interval produces a smaller response or none: the membrane is refractory until $h$ and $n$ return to their resting values.

04ion flux

From membrane current to ion flux

An ionic current is a flow of charged ions across the membrane. For monovalent ions, a current density $I$ through a patch of area $A$ moves

$$\Delta N = -\frac{I\,A\,\Delta t}{e\,n_p}$$

particles in a time step $\Delta t$, where $e$ is the elementary charge and $n_p = 1000$ is the number of ions represented by one particle. The model uses $A = 10^{-8}\,\text{cm}^2$ ($1\,\mu\text{m}^2$). Outward current is positive in the Hodgkin-Huxley convention, so the minus sign makes $\Delta N$ positive for influx. This flux drives the particle transport in section 01.

Each ion has three pathways: its voltage-gated channel, a leak, and the pump. The model divides the Hodgkin-Huxley leak into a $\text{Na}^+$ and a $\text{K}^+$ component and adds the pump current $I_p$, one net positive charge outward per cycle. The division and the resting pump current $I_{p0}$ satisfy

$$\begin{gathered} g_\text{L,Na}(V - E_\text{Na}) + g_\text{L,K}(V - E_\text{K}) + I_{p0} = g_\text{L}(V - E_\text{L}) \\ g_\text{L,Na} + g_\text{L,K} = g_\text{L} \end{gathered}$$

so with $I_p = I_{p0}$ the membrane equation is unchanged. A pump moving three $\text{Na}^+$ out and two $\text{K}^+$ in per cycle balances the passive currents at rest when $I_\text{Na,pass} = -3 I_{p0}$ and $I_\text{K,pass} = 2 I_{p0}$. At the resting state this gives $g_\text{L,Na} = 0.081$, $g_\text{L,K} = 0.219\,\text{mS/cm}^2$ and $I_{p0} = 3.52\,\mu\text{A/cm}^2$. The pump rate is taken proportional to the intracellular $\text{Na}^+$ content, a linearisation of its dependence on $[\text{Na}^+]_i$:

$$I_p = I_{p0}\,\frac{[\text{Na}^+]_i}{[\text{Na}^+]_{i,0}}$$

The plot shows the passive (channel and leak) $\text{Na}^+$ influx and $\text{K}^+$ efflux, each positive in its physiological direction. The sodium influx begins first, with the upstroke, and the potassium efflux follows as $n$ rises. For much of the spike the two flow at the same time and at similar rates, so a large part of the sodium charge entering the cell is offset by potassium charge leaving it.

At the peak, $I_\text{Na}$ reaches about $-800\,\mu\text{A/cm}^2$, which corresponds to about 50 particles, or $5 \times 10^4$ ions, per millisecond through the patch.

After an action potential the raised $[\text{Na}^+]_i$ speeds up the pump. Its extra outward current hyperpolarises the membrane by up to about a millivolt, which slightly reduces the passive $\text{K}^+$ efflux, so the pump restores both gradients even though it moves $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{K}^+$ in a 3:2 ratio. The $I_p$ readout shows the pump current returning to $I_{p0}$.
