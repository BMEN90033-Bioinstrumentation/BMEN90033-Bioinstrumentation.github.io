The action potential in a membrane patch

The resting potential of an excitable cell arises from ion concentration gradients across the membrane and from the selective permeability of the membrane to each ion. A brief depolarising stimulus opens voltage-gated sodium channels; the regenerative sodium influx that follows, ended by sodium inactivation and a delayed potassium efflux, produces the action potential. This page runs the Hodgkin-Huxley model of a membrane patch and shows the channel gating and ion flux that underlie the membrane potential.

01the membrane patch

Ion gradients, channels and the pump

The scene shows a cross-section of a lipid bilayer separating the extracellular fluid (above) from the cytoplasm (below). Sodium is concentrated outside the cell and potassium inside. The concentrations used for the readouts are typical mammalian values:

  • $\text{Na}^+$: $145\,\text{mM}$ outside, $15\,\text{mM}$ inside;
  • $\text{K}^+$: $5\,\text{mM}$ outside, $140\,\text{mM}$ inside.

Three membrane proteins span the bilayer. The voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ channel has four subunits that open with the activation variable $m$ and a cytoplasmic inactivation gate that moves into the pore as $h$ falls. The voltage-gated $\text{K}^+$ channel has four subunits that open with $n$. The $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase in a cell exports three $\text{Na}^+$ and imports two $\text{K}^+$ per ATP hydrolysed, restoring the gradients.

The pump motion in the scene is illustrative only: the model contains no pump current, so ions cross the membrane only through the two channels, at the rate set by the Hodgkin-Huxley currents (section 04).

Each rendered particle represents 1000 ions crossing a $1\,\mu\text{m}^2$ patch. The concentration readouts are computed from the particle count in each compartment. A single action potential changes them negligibly, so $E_\text{Na}$ and $E_\text{K}$ are held fixed. Because the resting leak is not balanced by a pump current, the readouts drift slowly over time.

02the membrane equation

The membrane as a capacitor with parallel conductances

The Hodgkin-Huxley model represents a patch of membrane as a capacitance $C_m$ in parallel with three ionic branches, each a conductance in series with the reversal potential of its ion. Conservation of charge gives

$$C_m \frac{dV}{dt} = I_\text{stim} - I_\text{Na} - I_\text{K} - I_\text{L}$$
$$\begin{aligned} I_\text{Na} &= \bar g_\text{Na}\, m^3 h\,(V - E_\text{Na}) \\ I_\text{K} &= \bar g_\text{K}\, n^4\,(V - E_\text{K}) \\ I_\text{L} &= g_\text{L}\,(V - E_\text{L}) \end{aligned}$$

The parameters are the standard squid-axon values with the resting potential near $-65\,\text{mV}$:

  • $C_m = 1\,\mu\text{F/cm}^2$;
  • $\bar g_\text{Na} = 120$, $\bar g_\text{K} = 36$, $g_\text{L} = 0.3\,\text{mS/cm}^2$;
  • $E_\text{Na} = 50$, $E_\text{K} = -77$, $E_\text{L} = -54.387\,\text{mV}$.

The reversal potentials are fixed model parameters. They are not recomputed from the mammalian concentrations in section 01 with the Nernst equation, $E_X = \frac{RT}{zF}\ln\frac{[X]_o}{[X]_i}$.

The equations are integrated by the forward Euler method with $\Delta t = 0.05\,\text{ms}$, four steps per animation frame. The stimulus is a rectangular current pulse of $1\,\text{ms}$. With these settings a pulse of about $7\,\mu\text{A/cm}^2$ or more produces a full action potential that peaks near $+40\,\text{mV}$ and undershoots to about $-76\,\text{mV}$. A weaker pulse produces a small depolarisation that decays passively.

Above threshold the peak voltage changes by only a few millivolts with stimulus strength: the response is all-or-none. During the upstroke $V_m$ approaches $E_\text{Na}$, and during the after-hyperpolarisation it approaches $E_\text{K}$.

03channel gating

Voltage-dependent gating variables

Each gating variable $x \in \{m, h, n\}$ is the fraction of its gates in the permissive state. It relaxes towards a voltage-dependent steady state at voltage-dependent rates $\alpha_x$ (opening) and $\beta_x$ (closing), in $\text{ms}^{-1}$ with $V$ in mV:

$$\frac{dx}{dt} = \alpha_x(V)\,(1 - x) - \beta_x(V)\,x$$
$$\begin{aligned} \alpha_m &= \frac{0.1\,(V + 40)}{1 - e^{-(V + 40)/10}}, & \beta_m &= 4\,e^{-(V + 65)/18} \\[4pt] \alpha_h &= 0.07\,e^{-(V + 65)/20}, & \beta_h &= \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(V + 35)/10}} \\[4pt] \alpha_n &= \frac{0.01\,(V + 55)}{1 - e^{-(V + 55)/10}}, & \beta_n &= 0.125\,e^{-(V + 65)/80} \end{aligned}$$

The three variables act on different time scales, which sets the sequence of the action potential:

  • $m$ is fast. A depolarisation raises $m$, $g_\text{Na} = \bar g_\text{Na} m^3 h$ increases, and the inward sodium current depolarises the membrane further. This positive feedback is the upstroke.
  • $h$ is slower and falls with depolarisation, inactivating the sodium conductance.
  • $n$ is also slow and rises with depolarisation, so $g_\text{K} = \bar g_\text{K} n^4$ grows after the upstroke and the outward potassium current repolarises the membrane.
After an action potential $h$ is still low and $n$ still high. A second stimulus delivered during this interval produces a smaller response or none: the membrane is refractory until $h$ and $n$ return to their resting values.

04ion flux

From membrane current to ion flux

An ionic current is a flow of charged ions across the membrane. For monovalent ions, a current density $I$ through a patch of area $A$ moves

$$\Delta N = -\frac{I\,A\,\Delta t}{e\,n_p}$$

particles in a time step $\Delta t$, where $e$ is the elementary charge and $n_p = 1000$ is the number of ions represented by one particle. The model uses $A = 10^{-8}\,\text{cm}^2$ ($1\,\mu\text{m}^2$). Outward current is positive in the Hodgkin-Huxley convention, so the minus sign makes $\Delta N$ positive for influx. This flux drives the particle transport in section 01.

The plot shows the $\text{Na}^+$ influx and the $\text{K}^+$ efflux, each positive in its physiological direction. The sodium influx begins first, with the upstroke, and the potassium efflux follows as $n$ rises. For much of the spike the two flow at the same time and at similar rates, so a large part of the sodium charge entering the cell is offset by potassium charge leaving it.

At the peak, $I_\text{Na}$ reaches about $-800\,\mu\text{A/cm}^2$, which corresponds to about 50 particles, or $5 \times 10^4$ ions, per millisecond through the patch.

The ions exchanged in one action potential are few compared with the ion content of the cell, so the reversal potentials are effectively unchanged. Over many action potentials the $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ pump restores the gradients.
