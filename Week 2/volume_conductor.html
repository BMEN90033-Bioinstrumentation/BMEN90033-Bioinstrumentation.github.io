BMEN90033 · WEEK 2 · VOLUME CONDUCTION

The volume conductor problem

Biopotentials such as the ECG and EEG are recorded at the body surface, some distance from the cells that generate them. Between source and electrode lies a volume conductor: blood, muscle, fat, bone and skin, each with its own conductivity. This page develops the field of a multipole source in that medium, the effect of layered tissue on each spatial harmonic, and the consequence for the signal measured between two electrodes.

01the forward problem

A multipole source in a volume conductor

The forward problem asks what potential a known current source produces at the body surface. In an infinite homogeneous medium of conductivity $\sigma$, a current dipole of moment $p$ at the origin produces

$$\Phi(r,\theta) = \frac{p\cos\theta}{4\pi\sigma r^2}$$

where $\theta$ is measured from the dipole axis. A cardiac or cortical source is not a pure dipole. Its potential is a sum of multipole terms of increasing order $l$, each with the angular pattern of a Legendre polynomial $P_l$:

$$\Phi(r,\theta) = \sum_{l=1}^{4} a_l\,\frac{P_l(\cos\theta)}{r^{\,l+1}}, \qquad \mathbf{E} = -\nabla\Phi$$

The dipole ($l = 1$) falls as $1/r^2$, the quadrupole ($l = 2$) as $1/r^3$ and the octupole ($l = 3$) as $1/r^4$, so higher-order terms decay faster with distance from the source.

  • blue arrows: the field in a homogeneous medium, the "true" field of the source;
  • pink arrows: the field with the enabled tissue shells in place, the field the electrodes sample;
  • electrodes: $V_A - V_B$ between the first two electrodes forms the recorded lead. A click on an electrode attaches a gizmo for moving it.
The model scales the source weights of the $l \ge 2$ terms by $R^{\,l-1}$, with $R = 14$ the electrode radius, and by a roll-off of $0.7^{\,l-2}$. Without this compensation the $l = 4$ term would be about 2700 times weaker than the dipole at the electrodes and its filtering would not be visible. 02tissue layers

Concentric shells of different conductivity

The torso and the head are not homogeneous. A standard approximation represents them as concentric spherical shells, each with a uniform conductivity. At every boundary the potential and the normal current density are continuous:

$$\Phi_k = \Phi_{k+1}, \qquad \sigma_k \frac{\partial \Phi_k}{\partial r} = \sigma_{k+1} \frac{\partial \Phi_{k+1}}{\partial r} \qquad \text{at } r = R_k$$

Within shell $k$ each multipole term takes the form

$$\Phi_k = \left[A_k\, r^{\,l} + B_k\, r^{-(l+1)}\right] P_l(\cos\theta)$$

The innermost shell holds the source, so its $r^{-(l+1)}$ coefficient is that of the homogeneous solution. The outermost enabled shell extends to infinity, so its $r^{\,l}$ coefficient is zero. The boundary conditions then fix every other coefficient (Rush and Driscoll, 1968).

  • The model conductivities range from $1.8\,\text{S/m}$ for cerebrospinal fluid to $0.001\,\text{S/m}$ for skin.
  • Low-conductivity layers such as lung, fat and skull lie between the source and the electrodes.
  • The ECG preset encloses the heart in blood, lung, muscle, fat and skin; the EEG preset encloses the brain in CSF, skull and scalp.
The skull is about 40 times less conductive than the scalp in this model. Current from a cortical source spreads laterally in the CSF and scalp before reaching the electrodes, which is the main reason EEG potentials are spatially smeared.

03spatial filtering

The volume conductor as a spatial low-pass filter

The shells multiply each multipole term by a transfer coefficient $T_l$. For a single boundary between the source medium $\sigma_\text{in}$ and an outer medium $\sigma_\text{out}$,

$$T_l = \frac{(2l+1)\,\sigma_\text{in}}{l\,\sigma_\text{in} + (l+1)\,\sigma_\text{out}}$$

The amplitude of each term at the electrode radius $R$ is then

$$|\Phi_l(R)| \propto \frac{a_l\, T_l}{R^{\,l+1}}$$

Two effects act together. The geometric factor $1/R^{\,l+1}$ already favours the dipole, and for the layered models $T_l$ falls as $l$ rises, so the quadrupole and octupole terms lose more amplitude than the dipole term. Higher multipole orders carry finer angular detail, so the volume conductor acts as a spatial low-pass filter.

The plot normalises both series to their own dipole term. The difference between the blue and pink bars is the additional, order-dependent attenuation caused by the tissue. The overall gain $T_1$ is reported separately; it exceeds 1 when a poorly conducting layer such as skin is outermost, because that layer confines the current.

Surface electrodes therefore record a smoothed image of the source. Fine spatial structure in the source distribution is attenuated before it reaches the skin, which is why a single equivalent dipole describes much of the surface ECG.

04the recorded signal

Quasi-static conduction: scaling without temporal filtering

Below about $1\,\text{kHz}$ tissue behaves as a resistive medium: capacitive currents and propagation delays are negligible. The potential then satisfies Poisson's equation at each instant,

$$\nabla \cdot \left(\sigma \nabla \Phi\right) = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J}_s$$

where $\mathbf{J}_s$ is the impressed source current density. The surface potential follows the source without delay and without frequency-dependent filtering (Plonsey and Heppner, 1967). The tissue changes only the spatial weighting of the source.

  • pure dipole (source complexity 0): every electrode at $R = 14$ sees the same factor $T_1$, so the recorded trace is an exact scaled copy of the true trace.
  • multipole source: each order is scaled by a different $T_l$, so the ratio of the two traces changes as the source direction changes and the recorded waveform differs in shape.
  • ECG: the dipole traces a vector loop over a $0.8\,\text{s}$ cycle ($75\,\text{bpm}$), giving P, QRS and T deflections.
  • EEG: a mainly radial cortical dipole oscillates with components from $2.5$ to $35\,\text{Hz}$, dominated by the alpha band near $10\,\text{Hz}$.
Selecting ECG or EEG also loads the matching tissue preset. The display scale multiplies only the plotted recorded trace, so that a strongly attenuated signal can be compared in shape with the true signal; the attenuation readout is unaffected by it.
