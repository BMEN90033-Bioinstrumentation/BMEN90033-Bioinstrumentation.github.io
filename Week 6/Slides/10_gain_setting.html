If the closed-loop gain is set by R2/R1, do the individual values of R2 and R1 matter?

Correct answer: B. The gain equation ACL = 1 + R2/R1 depends only on the ratio, but practical constraints set a useful range, typically 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ.

Too low (< 1 kΩ). The op-amp output must supply a large current to low-value resistors. Most op-amps can source only about 20 mA to 25 mA, so small resistances cause the output to clip, distort or overheat.

Too high (> 1 MΩ). Input bias currents (nA to μA) flowing through large resistances create significant DC offset voltages at the output. Stray board capacitance (of the order of pF) in parallel with high-value resistors forms unintended low-pass filters that limit bandwidth. Johnson (thermal) noise also scales with √R.

Values from 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ generally keep the output current, bias-current offset, thermal noise and the effect of parasitic capacitance small.
