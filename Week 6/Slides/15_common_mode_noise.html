Common-mode signal
A voltage that appears identically on both inputs, for example 50 Hz mains interference. Because it is equal on and , subtraction cancels it.
Differential signal
The voltage difference between the two inputs is the wanted signal, for example a 1 mV ECG. An instrumentation amplifier amplifies only this difference.
Common-mode rejection ratio
The ratio of differential gain to common-mode gain. It measures how strongly the amplifier rejects common-mode noise relative to the wanted differential signal. A higher CMRR leaves less interference at the output.
